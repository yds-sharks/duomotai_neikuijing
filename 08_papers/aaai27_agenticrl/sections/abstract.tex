\begin{abstract}
Multimodal RAG fails when the initial candidate pool lacks answer-supporting
evidence: selection can remove distractors, but cannot recover evidence that
was never retrieved. We study how a lightweight controller can preserve useful
evidence and redirect retrieval over a fixed budget while the retrievers,
deployed index, and generator remain frozen. We present AgenticRL, a
closed-loop framework in which one policy jointly selects candidate evidence
and chooses whether to preserve the current evidence or execute a rewritten
query. The inference wrapper separates generation-visible retained evidence
from controller-only failed retrieval traces, preventing unsuccessful search
history from contaminating the final answer context. During training, rewritten
queries are executed against the deployed index, and both preservation and
rewrite actions are evaluated on a shared utility scale derived from the frozen
generator. This grounds controller learning in the actual behavior of the
retriever, index, and generator without updating any of them. We optimize the
controller with an SFT--RFT--GRPO curriculum using retrieval-need-aware
sampling and forced rewrite exploration. On all 6,832 questions of EndoBench,
AgenticRL reaches 42.84\% accuracy, improving the same frozen generator by
2.25 percentage points and single-round control by 2.72 points
($p<0.0001$). It corrects 10.66\% of single-round errors, with the largest
gains on spatial and procedural reasoning. Its accuracy is also 1.15 points
above the 41.69\% GPT-4o result reported by EndoBench, although this comparison
is secondary because AgenticRL uses an external index. Matched ablations show a
positive but non-significant 1.30-point contribution from cross-round state,
so the strongest evidence supports the complete fixed-budget,
evidence-preserving control strategy rather than a protocol-independent memory
effect.
\end{abstract}
